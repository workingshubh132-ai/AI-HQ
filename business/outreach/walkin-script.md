# Walk-in Opener — DRAFT

> **DRAFT. Rewrite in your own words before using.** A message that
> sounds like a template is worse than no message. This is a structure,
> not a script to read aloud. Nothing here has been sent to anyone.
> Preferred channel per `docs/EXPERIMENT-001.md` §6.

## The opening

> "Hi — are you the owner? I'm [name], I'm local. I was looking up
> [service] in [area] and noticed something about how you show up
> online that's probably costing you calls. Took me a couple of minutes
> to check. Can I show you? It's one page, and there's no charge for
> it."

Then hand over the printed audit. Say nothing while they read it. **Let
them ask what it costs** — don't offer the price first.

## If they're busy

> "No problem — can I just leave this with you? My number's on it. If
> it's useful, call me; if not, no worries either way."

## If they ask what it costs before you've shown the audit

> "Nothing for this — it's just what I found. If you want it fixed after
> reading it, that's a separate, fixed price. No pressure."

## What makes this work, and what doesn't

The whole pitch stands or falls on the first sentence containing a fact
about *them* specifically — the finding from
`prospects/qualification-checklist.md`. Everything after that is
replaceable. Generic small talk before the finding loses attention fast.

Do not lead with your own credentials, your business name, or a general
pitch about "helping local businesses." Lead with what you found.
